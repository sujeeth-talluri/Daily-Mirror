Living Around Broken Walls

COMPLACENCY · COMPASSION

Every year after the monsoon, our roads develop potholes. Every day, thousands of people drive through those same roads.

They slow down.

They avoid the potholes.

They complain.

Then they continue with the rest of their day.

The next day...
they do exactly the same thing again.

After a while, those potholes become so familiar that people simply learn to live with them. Recently, I came across a group of people who had lost their own family members in pothole-related accidents. They had every reason to keep blaming the authorities. Instead, they chose a different response. They spent their own money.

Picked up shovels.

Filled potholes wherever they could. Not because it was their responsibility. But because they didn't want another family to go through the same pain they had experienced. That made me stop and think. Some people become experts at living around broken things. Others quietly begin rebuilding them.

🪞 The Mirror

Looking into my own mirror...

I wonder how many broken walls I've simply learned to live around. Not because I agree with them. But because they've been there for so long. It's surprisingly easy to become an expert at pointing out what's broken. It's much harder to become someone who helps rebuild it. Perhaps that's why real change rarely begins with a crowd. It usually begins with one person who decides,

"I may not be able to rebuild the whole wall... but I can rebuild the part that's in front of me."

Maybe that's the question I need to ask myself.

What have I become so comfortable complaining about...
that I've forgotten I could become part of rebuilding it?

💭 Think about it...

What broken wall have I simply learned to live around? Where have I contributed more complaints than solutions?

What is one brick I can lay today...
instead of waiting for someone else to rebuild the whole wall?
