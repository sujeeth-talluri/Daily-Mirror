What Never Changed

PRESENCE · TRUST

A few days ago, I was talking to one of the college interns in my team who had just completed her first month of internship. I asked her how things were going. She smiled and said,

"It's tough."
Everything was new.
A new office.
New colleagues.
A different city.

The pressure of her very first job. For the first time in her life, she was away from her family.

She missed home.

Listening to her took me back almost nineteen years. I still remember my first job. Then later, moving to the United States for the first time.

A new country.
A different culture.
Unknown people.

Everything around me felt unfamiliar. Looking back now, I realise something. It wasn't that those places were bad. They were simply unfamiliar. And unfamiliar places have a way of making us question whether we really belong there. Over time, those unfamiliar places slowly became familiar. The people became friends. The work became routine. The place eventually felt like home.

Then life moved me somewhere else...
and the whole process began again.

🪞 The Mirror

Looking into my own mirror...

I realise how much we naturally long for familiarity. Whenever life changes, we immediately start searching for something that feels like home.

A familiar face.
A familiar voice.
A familiar routine.
A familiar place.

Perhaps that's why unfamiliar seasons often feel so unsettling.

Not because they're always worse...
but because we've never walked through them before.

As I look back today, through every unfamiliar season of my life, I realise there was one thing that never became unfamiliar. The presence that quietly walked with me.

Places changed.
People changed.
Responsibilities changed.
Countries changed.
Seasons changed.

But there was one thing that never changed. Perhaps that's why I was able to keep moving forward.

Not because I always knew what tomorrow would look like...
but because I never had to walk into tomorrow alone.

💭 Think about it...

What unfamiliar season of life am I struggling to embrace today?

Am I resisting it because it's truly the wrong place...
or simply because it doesn't feel like home yet?

What if I've been searching for something familiar around me...

while overlooking the One thing that has never stopped walking beside me?
