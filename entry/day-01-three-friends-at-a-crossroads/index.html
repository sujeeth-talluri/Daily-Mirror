Three Friends at a Crossroads

fearfalse-securitymilestone-vs-journeypostponement

Three friends arrived at a fork in the road.

The first looked around and saw a large crowd taking the left path.

"So many people can't be wrong," he thought.

Without much thought, he joined them.

Years later, he realized that most of the crowd had simply been following one another.

The second friend stood still.

He asked one person for advice.

Then another.

Then another.

Every person gave a different answer.

Afraid of making the wrong choice, he spent so much time listening that he never really moved forward.

The third friend carried a map that had guided him well for years.

Halfway through his journey, an older traveler stopped him.

"That map is old," he said. "I know a better route."

The advice sounded reasonable.

The traveler seemed experienced.

So he folded the map and followed him.

The road was pleasant at first.

But it slowly took him further and further away from where he wanted to go.

Now tell me.

Which friend made the biggest mistake?

The one who followed the crowd?

The one who listened to everyone?

Or the one who abandoned the map?

Perhaps all three made the same mistake.

They trusted every voice except the one that should have guided them.

Reflection

Every day we stand at our own crossroads.

Some voices appeal to popularity.

Some appeal to fear.

Some appeal to comfort.

Some simply tell us what we want to hear.

The direction of our life is often determined by a simple question:

Which voice have I given permission to guide me?
