The Guitar

SELF-EXAMINATION

While cleaning the house one weekend, a father opened an old cupboard. Behind a few boxes lay a guitar, covered with a thick layer of dust. His young son picked it up carefully.

"Dad... whose guitar is this?"
The father smiled.
"It used to be mine."

The boy looked surprised.

"You know how to play the guitar?"
"I used to."
"When was the last time you played it?"
The father paused.
He couldn't remember.

The boy gently wiped the dust from the strings. Then he looked at his dad and asked,

"So... you don't really have a guitar, do you?"
The father laughed.
"What do you mean? It's right there."
The boy smiled.
"No...
you only have the memory of one."

The father didn't laugh this time.

🪞 Mirror

Funny, isn't it?

Having something for namesake isn't the same as having it. An old guitar that hasn't been played for years is almost the same as not having one. A skill that hasn't been practised for years is almost the same as not having that skill. A cousin or sibling you haven't spoken to for years is almost the same as not having that relationship. A religious book that has sat unopened on a shelf for years is almost the same as not having it.

What we stop using...
What we stop nurturing...

Slowly fades from our lives. Not because someone took it away. But because we quietly left it untouched.

Think about it.

What do I still say I have... but have stopped using or nurturing for so long that I can't honestly say I really have it anymore?
