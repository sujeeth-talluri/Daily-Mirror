When Wrong Becomes Normal

COMPLACENCY · IGNORING WARNING SIGNS

Every morning on my way to the office, I cross a flyover in Hyderabad. Like many roads in the city, one side is meant for vehicles travelling in one direction. The other side is meant for vehicles coming from the opposite direction. Yet almost every day, I see people driving on the wrong side. Not one or two.

Dozens of them.

Sometimes at full speed. The surprising part isn't that it happens. The surprising part is that it has become so normal. In fact, if you patiently stay in your own lane and wait your turn, you're the one who almost looks different. A couple of weeks ago, that same everyday routine ended in tragedy. Two young men lost their lives in a head-on collision after riding on the wrong side. The road hadn't suddenly become dangerous that day. The danger had simply become ordinary.

🪞 The Mirror

Looking into my own mirror...
I wonder how many things in my life have become ordinary...
not because they're right...
but simply because they've been around me for so long.
The first time we see something wrong...
it disturbs us.
The next few times...
it doesn't feel as serious.
Eventually...
we stop noticing it altogether.

Not because the wrong changed. Because our hearts quietly adjusted to it. Perhaps that's one of life's greatest dangers. It's rarely one big decision that changes us.

It's the small compromises...
accepted often enough...
until they no longer feel like compromises.

Then one day we look around and ask,

"How did this become normal?"

Maybe it never became normal. Maybe I simply became familiar with it. Perhaps the harder question isn't,

"Has the world changed?"
Perhaps it's,
"Have I slowly accepted things that I once knew weren't right?"

💭 Think about it...

What have I become so familiar with...
that I no longer question it?

Where has familiarity slowly silenced my conscience?

Am I shaping my life by what has become common...
or by what I know is right?

Am I shaping my life by what has become common... or by what I know is right?
