It Ends With Me.

SELF-EXAMINATION

As a little girl, she would often ask herself,

"Why does a family have to be like this?"

She grew up watching ego win over relationships.

People smiled in front of each other...
and spoke differently once they walked away.

Arguments quickly became abusive. Nobody wanted to listen. Everyone wanted to win. As a little girl, she quietly made herself a promise.

"One day, when I have my own family... this is not how my home will be."
Years passed.
She got married.
Started a family.

She loved them deeply. Without even realizing it, some of the very things she hated as a child slowly became normal.

A harsh word.
A wounded ego.

The need to have the last word. Winning an argument instead of protecting the relationship. Then one evening, a disagreement with her husband became ugly. She lost her temper. Said things she never imagined she would say. She walked away and sat alone in the room.

For a long time...
there was only silence.

Then, one by one, memories from her childhood came rushing back.

The shouting.
The hurt.
The tears.

The promise she had made to herself as a little girl. And suddenly she saw it. She wasn't just remembering her childhood anymore. She was beginning to repeat it.

She stood up.

Walked towards the bedroom. Her hand rested on the door for a few seconds. This was no longer about winning an argument. This was about deciding what kind of family her children would grow up in.

🪞 The Mirror...

One thing I've observed in life is this.

We naturally repeat what we repeatedly live around...
unless we intentionally choose otherwise.

Every family passes something to the next generation. Some patterns deserve to be carried forward. Others deserve to end.

The question isn't,
"What kind of family did I grow up in?"
The question is,
"What kind of family will grow up because of me?"

Not every inheritance deserves another generation.

Maybe the greatest legacy I leave my children is this... they never have to make the promise I once made as a little girl.
