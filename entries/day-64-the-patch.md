As the monsoon season is slowly coming to an end in Hyderabad, many of the city's roads have started showing the damage left behind by weeks of heavy rain.

If you've driven around the city recently, you've probably noticed it too.

Potholes.

Broken patches.

Damaged stretches of road.

Soon enough, repair teams arrive.

Fresh tar is poured over the damaged section.

Traffic starts moving again.

From a distance...

the road looks perfectly fine.

For a few days...

it really is.

But after another spell of rain...

or a few days of heavy traffic...

the fresh layer begins to peel away.

The same pothole reappears.

Sometimes...

even worse than before.

Watching those roads made me realise something.

The patch wasn't useless.

It solved the immediate problem.

But it never solved the real one.

The damaged surface had been covered.

The damaged foundation remained.

Real repair would mean cutting deeper.

Removing what was weak.

Rebuilding what couldn't support the road anymore.

That takes more time.

More effort.

More inconvenience.

But it lasts.

🪞 The Mirror

Looking into my own mirror...

I wonder how often I settle for patches.

Not because I don't want change...

but because patches are quicker.

They're less painful.

They don't require me to dig beneath the surface.

How often do I silence the symptom...

instead of confronting the source?

I become busy...

instead of dealing with my loneliness.

I blame circumstances...

instead of examining my attitude.

I promise myself,

"I'll do better next time..."

without asking why I keep ending up in the same place.

Perhaps that's why some struggles never really leave.

They simply disappear for a while...

until life applies pressure again.

Then the same words.

The same reactions.

The same habits.

The same wounds.

Not because the problem came back...

but because it never truly left.

Maybe the hardest part of real change...

is that it asks us to deal with what nobody else can see.

Covering it is faster.

Digging into it is harder.

But only one of them actually heals.

💭 Think about it...

What in my life only looks repaired...

but still isn't restored?

Where have I been settling for temporary relief...

instead of lasting change?

Am I patching over the surface...

while quietly avoiding the foundation?
