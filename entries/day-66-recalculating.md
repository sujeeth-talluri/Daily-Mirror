These days, most of us switch on Google Maps whenever we drive.

Not because we don't know the destination...

but because it tells us the estimated time, the traffic ahead, and sometimes even a better route.

One situation, however, is familiar to almost every driver.

You miss a turn.

Or you accidentally take the flyover when you were supposed to stay on the service road.

The GPS doesn't stop and remind you that you missed the turn.

It doesn't say,

"I told you so."

It doesn't keep a record of how many times you've ignored its directions.

It simply pauses for a moment...

and says,

"Recalculating."

Then it immediately starts showing the best possible route from where you are now.

What fascinates me isn't the GPS.

It's often the people inside the car.

The GPS is already focused on reaching the destination.

The passengers are still discussing the missed turn.

"You should have taken that exit."

"I told you not to take the flyover."

"Now we've lost another ten minutes."

The GPS has already moved on.

The passengers haven't.

🪞 The Mirror

Looking into my own mirror...

I wonder how often I live like one of those passengers.

How much time do I spend replaying missed turns?

"If only I had..."

"I shouldn't have..."

"Why did that happen?"

"Whose fault was it?"

The more I replay yesterday...

the less attention I give to today.

The past may explain where I am.

But it doesn't have to decide where I go next.

Life rarely moves forward while I'm arguing with the road I've already missed.

It moves forward when I accept where I am...

and choose the next right step.

Perhaps that's why we remain stuck for so long.

Not because there isn't another route...

but because we're still standing at yesterday's missed turn.

The destination is never reached by replaying the mistake.

It's reached by recalculating.

💭 Think about it...

What missed turn in my life am I still replaying?

Have I spent more time asking, "Whose fault was it?"...

than asking, "What is my next right step?"

If I stopped looking in the rear-view mirror...

what new direction might I finally see?
