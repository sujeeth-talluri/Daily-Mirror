I was recently reading about a tragic fire at a nursing home in the United States.

Like many hospitals and corporate buildings, they regularly conducted fire drills to prepare people for emergencies.

Everyone knew what the alarm sounded like.

Everyone knew what they were supposed to do.

Then one day, a real fire broke out.

When the alarm went off, some people assumed it was just another drill.

Instead of evacuating immediately, they delayed responding.

By the time they realised the danger was real...

it was too late.

That incident has since become an important lesson in fire safety.

The greatest danger wasn't that people didn't hear the alarm.

It was that they had heard it so many times...

that they stopped responding to it.

🪞 The Mirror

Looking into my own mirror...

I wonder how many warnings in life have slowly become background noise.

Not because they aren't true...

but because I've heard them so often.

Take care of your health.

Spend time with your family.

Don't neglect your relationship with God.

Deal with that habit.

Make that phone call.

Forgive.

Change.

We've heard some of these warnings so many times...

that we've quietly convinced ourselves there will always be another opportunity.

Perhaps that's one of life's greatest dangers.

The first warning usually gets our attention.

The tenth warning...

we hardly notice.

Maybe the real question isn't,

"Did I hear the warning?"

Maybe it's,

"Have I become so familiar with it... that I no longer respond?"

💭 Think about it...

What warning have I heard so many times...

that it no longer moves me?

What have I been postponing...

assuming there will always be another chance?

Is there something I already know I need to act on...

today?
