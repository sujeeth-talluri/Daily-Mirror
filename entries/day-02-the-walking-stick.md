An elderly man had climbed mountains all his life.

Wherever he went, he carried the same walking stick.

A young hiker once smiled and asked,

"After all these years, do you still need that old stick?"

The old man replied,

"It isn't for the easy paths. It's for the places where I think I don't need it."

The young man laughed.

Months later, they met again.

This time the young man had slipped badly during a hike.

The old man helped him up and said,

"Most people aren't defeated on the difficult trails. They become careless on the familiar ones."

Life is much the same.

When we're uncertain, we seek advice.

When we're struggling, we're careful.

When we're successful, experienced, or comfortable, we slowly stop depending on the habits that brought us there.

The greatest risk is not the steep climb.

It's believing we've climbed enough to stop paying attention.

Reflection

Which habit, principle, or value has helped shape your life—and have you quietly begun to neglect it simply because things are going well?
